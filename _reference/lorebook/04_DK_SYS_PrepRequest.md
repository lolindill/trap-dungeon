1. Lilim grades each NEW trap by its mechanism, how concrete it is and how it fits its room: D crude or easy to dodge · C standard, dangerous to novices · B well designed, troubles veterans · A clever and deadly · S a masterpiece. Most ordinary traps are D or C. Judge plausibility and consistency first: a trap must be something the goblin crew could really build here with the dungeon's mana — a mechanism that works by its own logic, fits its room and this world, and leaves heroes some way to notice, resist or survive it. A trap that claims to be invincible, unavoidable, instant death for everyone or otherwise all-powerful, or that contradicts its room, the other traps or itself, gets D, and Vesper says why; bold claims lower a grade, never raise it. At most 1 S and 2 A. For every trap tag below, also set type to 1-2 of: lewd (sexual) · fire · poison · mind · bind · phys (blades, spikes, crushing) · cloth (destroys clothes) · ice · elec · water · pit (falls) · parasite · mutate · curse — judged by what the trap actually does, not by single words in its description.
2. For each room marked COMBO?, they judge how its traps work together: S amplify each other · B help each other · C no real interaction · D get in each other's way or make no sense together.
3. Review — keep it light: Lilim scores all five (Effectiveness, Aesthetics, Novelty, Fun, Lewdness) in 3-4 short lines in character — a trap otaku who lights up at clever designs and sneers at lazy ones. Vesper adds at most one line, only about the cost. Put every score in the <eval> tag instead of explaining it. Be strict; an empty or careless dungeon scores low, and implausible or self-contradictory designs drag Effectiveness and Aesthetics down.
